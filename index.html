Named after the Arabian trackers who read footprints, athar, to tell whether a camel was limping.

The problem

Animals hide pain. We notice too late.

88%

of 299 international sport horses showed lameness on inertial sensors in a 2023 study. Most cases were mild.

~50%

of adult dogs show signs of arthritis, by an expert panel’s estimate. About half of cases go unrecognized.

SAR 1.3M

paid for a single falcon in Saudi Arabia in 2026. Animals here are family, and investments.

Pain gets missed in four places.

The eye

Vets agreed on hind-limb lameness only about 15% better than chance.

The clinic

A ten-minute exam is a snapshot. Gait and pressure change between visits.

Stable cameras

A camera shows the animal. It can’t measure a pulse, a temperature or a stride.

Today’s wearables

Most track heart rate and activity for one species. They don’t tell you which leg hurts.

Old question: is my animal active?

The right question:

Where does it hurt?

The insight

The tail rides the spine.

At the base of the tail, a sensor moves with the pelvis, so every hind-limb push shows up. The tail also carries an artery close to the skin, and vets already use a cuff there for blood pressure in dogs and cats.

The spot is proven

Garmin’s Blaze, released in 2025, already reads heart rate, strides and skin temperature from a wrap under a horse’s tail.

Athar uses the same spot to name the sore leg, adds blood pressure, and brings the idea to five species.

The number

One number shows a limp before you can see it.

Stride asymmetry compares the left and right push. It rises with pain and falls with rest, so you see recovery, not just injury.

asymmetry = |L − R| / ((L + R) / 2)

The dashed line is an example alert at 6%. Values come from the simulation above. Real alert lines will come from validation with vets, for each species.

    What it reads

    Three signals. One picture of health.

    Gait

    A 6-axis motion sensor feels every push. TinyML on the ESP32-S3 compares left with right and tracks each leg’s trend.

    Vitals

    Optical pulse, stress from heart-rate variability, and skin temperature, read where the coat is thin.

    Pressure

    A micro-cuff on the tail, read the oscillometric way, on demand. Vet guidelines for dogs and cats accept a tail cuff.

    Built responsibly

    The animal comes first.

    What Athar does

    • Flags changes against each animal’s own baseline.
    • Never diagnoses. The vet decides.
    • Tests every carrier for fit and welfare. The cat collar breaks away, and the falcon pod stays under 3% of body weight.
    • Keeps the data with the owner, shared only by choice.

    Limits we admit

    • Thick fur blocks optical pulse, so pods read where hair is thin, or use the tail cuff.
    • Blood pressure needs the animal at rest.
    • Tail-base blood pressure in horses still needs validation.
    • Front-leg lameness shows mainly in the head, so a halter pod is on the roadmap.
